# Storyline and Purpose

This document is the narrative compass for future updates.

## Core statement

MyDrugs is about the difference between using substances to escape yourself and using altered states, recovery, and integration to return to yourself.

The player should begin by discovering that drugs are useful and exciting. The player can become powerful, efficient, productive, and creative. The mod should let that fantasy breathe.

The danger is not that every drug is automatically evil. The danger is that the player may start solving every problem through stronger substances, stronger machines, stronger mixes, and stronger body modifications. The mod should show how that becomes a loop.

The healing path is not abstinence-as-punishment. It is recovery, reflection, habits, preparation, psychedelics, integration, and finally freedom.

## The main conflict

The main conflict is:

```text
Escape vs integration
```

Not:

```text
Drugs vs sobriety
```

Some substances support work, focus, calm, or exploration. Some routes become addictive or harmful. Psychedelics are different: they can open insight and inner-world healing, but only when the player prepares, integrates, and respects the process.

## Player emotional arc

### 1. Discovery

The player finds plants and simple substances.

Feeling:

> This is weird, useful, and fun.

Gameplay:

- crops;
- primitive processing;
- first effects;
- first knowledge;
- simple risks.

### 2. Control

The player builds machines and recipes.

Feeling:

> I can master this.

Gameplay:

- drying, grinding, distilling, reacting, filtering;
- energy systems;
- pipes;
- JEI-driven recipes;
- the guide pushes progression;
- the player becomes efficient.

### 3. Temptation

The player sees that stronger routes are easier, faster, or more powerful.

Feeling:

> I can push through anything if I have the right substance.

Gameplay:

- stimulants;
- dangerous mixes;
- overclocking;
- crash and withdrawal;
- body strain;
- early body modification.

This stage should tempt the player. It should not force every player into addiction.

### 4. The limit

The player starts seeing that control is not the same as healing.

Feeling:

> My machines are stronger, but I am not necessarily better.

Gameplay:

- addiction pressure;
- diary entries become more reflective;
- recovery systems become necessary;
- the guide may still push optimization;
- the diary begins asking why the player is doing it.

### 5. Psychedelic opening

LSD and mushrooms open a different path.

Feeling:

> This is not another buff. This is showing me something.

Gameplay:

- altered perception;
- ore aura / hidden paths;
- safer ritual clarity;
- inner-world hints;
- dream-state access;
- Psychotrope Resonator unlock.

### 6. Integration

The player learns that the trip is not the cure. Integration is.

Feeling:

> The experience gave me a door. Now I have to do the work.

Gameplay:

- diary objectives;
- Recovery Sanctuary upgrades;
- therapy-like actions;
- diet/exercise/sleep/music;
- integration materials;
- Resonator recipes;
- addiction recovery support.

### 7. Inner Dimension

The dimension is a manifestation of the player’s inside.

Feeling:

> This place is beautiful, frightening, and mine.

Gameplay:

- sky islands;
- symbolic biomes;
- memory fragments;
- addiction/craving regions;
- peaceful guides;
- hostile psychological fragments;
- Mystical Ores replacing amethyst in advanced healing recipes.

### 8. Confrontation

The boss is not “drugs are bad.” The boss is the loop itself.

Feeling:

> I understand why this thing protected me. I also understand why I cannot obey it forever.

Gameplay:

- final boss uses addiction, diary, recovery, dimension, and psychedelic mechanics;
- the fight is won through integration, not only damage;
- recovery progress matters.

### 9. Return

The player returns freer.

Feeling:

> I can play again without being ruled by the system.

Gameplay rewards:

- easier recovery;
- safer trips;
- stable Resonator use;
- endgame armor / flight;
- ore aura / fortune-like perception;
- post-boss freedom;
- optional challenge, not mandatory chores.

## Guide vs Diary

The guide and diary should not have the same voice.

### Field Guide

The guide is mechanical and goal-oriented.

It says:

- what to build;
- what to process;
- what is missing;
- what unlocks next;
- how to avoid soft-locks.

It can be seductive because progression itself is seductive.

### Diary

The diary is reflective.

It says:

- what the player is becoming;
- whether the player is escaping or integrating;
- what recovery step matters next;
- what the current addiction/stress/body state means;
- how the player can return to balance.

The guide tells the player how to continue. The diary asks whether continuing this way is helping.

## Meth after LSD

The current progression may place meth after LSD. This can work only if meth is framed as a false mastery branch, not as the true spiritual endgame.

Meth answers:

> How do I force myself to keep going?

LSD and mushrooms answer:

> Why am I forcing myself to keep going?

The diary should eventually guide the player back from overclocking/body-strain progression toward integration.

## Positive ending rule

The end of the mod should not trap the player in more maintenance.

Post-boss systems should make the player more free:

- fewer forced recovery chores;
- better tools for managing addiction;
- safer access to the Inner Dimension;
- stable Resonator workflows;
- meaningful but optional high-risk routes.

The ending should say:

> You are not magically cured. But you are no longer ruled by the loop.
